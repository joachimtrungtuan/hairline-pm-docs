Why these routes exist Three patients the app cannot serve today

Not everyone opening this app wants surgery.

Today every route ends in a treatment inquiry. These three people need something else first.

“My hair is thinning. I don’t know if it’s getting worse, and I’m nowhere near ready to talk about surgery.”

Needs a record of what is actually happening over months — and, if they choose it, a professional opinion without committing to a treatment.

02 Monitor Hair Loss

“I had my transplant. Now I’m on my own, and every week looks the same to me.”

Needs a private recovery log with no obligation, no clinician to wait for, and a way to compare month one against month four.

03 Monitor Transplant Progress

“I want someone to actually guide my recovery — tell me what to do and when.”

Needs a paid, managed plan: a named provider, milestones, medication reminders and scheduled check-ins.

04 Request Aftercare

Same app, three different promises Only one of the three ends in a sale
How to read this deck Nine words that carry the whole model

The vocabulary, in plain language.

Platform

Three separate workspaces: Patient app, Provider portal, Admin console. “Cross-platform” means an action in one changes what another sees.

Case

One open episode of a service, with its own timeline, data and status. A patient may hold only one active case per route.

Daily log

One dated entry made by the patient: severity 1–10, free notes, optional photos.

Scan set

A fixed group of head photos taken from prescribed angles, so two different months can actually be compared.

Advice gate

The medical questionnaire a patient must complete before any provider is allowed to advise them. No questionnaire, no advice.

Cadence

How often the assigned provider is expected to respond — weekly or twice-monthly.

Assignment

Admin choosing the provider who will serve a case. Nothing provider-side exists until this happens.

Conversion

Carrying a monitoring case into a paid path — a treatment inquiry or an aftercare package — without the patient re-typing anything.

FR-0xx

The requirement document a screen comes from: 037 hair loss, 038 transplant progress, 011 aftercare, 003 inquiry, 025 questionnaire.

Case states Active → Pending assignment → Advice active → Pending reassignment → Completed → Converted

The states that get forgotten Six screens nobody remembers to brief

Most of the design risk is in the unhappy paths.

Duplicate case A second case on the same route

Only one active case per route is allowed, so the system blocks creation instead of opening a new one.

Needs a real screen offering “resume monitoring” or “end the existing case” — not an error toast.

Conversion abandoned The patient leaves mid-conversion

Nothing is half-created. The monitoring case stays active and the conversion draft is preserved.

Needs a resumable draft that is obviously not yet a purchase.

Provider withdraws The assigned clinician steps away

Access is revoked, the case is marked pending reassignment, and both Admin and patient are told.

Needs a patient-facing waiting state where logging visibly still works.

Not yet assigned Advice requested, nobody assigned

The gap between choosing advice and Admin assigning a provider can last days.

The dashboard must be genuinely useful with the advice panel empty.

Payment incomplete Aftercare checkout fails or expires

No payment means no request, no assignment and no plan — but the intake work was real.

Needs a recovery path back to purchase that keeps everything already entered.

Admin correction A value is edited after the fact

Admin may correct permitted fields with a reason; the original is kept as an immutable audit event.

Needs a way to show the corrected value without hiding that it was corrected.

Drawn from the alternative and exception flows of FR-011, FR-037 and FR-038 Each one is a state, not an error
